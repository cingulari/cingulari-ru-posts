Let’s cut to the chase: I’ll state my main point right away -- ultimately, we’re being coerced into trading away the last vestiges of internet freedom for the sake of a phantom "protection" of children—who, incidentally, have no business being online in the first place. ## Okay, can you give more details?

Sure. Just keep in mind that I’m not exactly a reliable source, so double-check everything yourself. I’m just passing on what I’ve heard -- relying on the general consensus I’ve seen on Telegram and YouTube. Besides, Britain isn’t Europe, and -blah, blah, blah- just lemme shitpost a little.

In July of this year, Europe began gradually [introducing mandatory age verification](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eu-age-verification), of course, to protect children. To prove that you’ve reached the age of majority, you’re required to show relevant documentation, such as a passport, driver’s license, or some other official ID. Furthermore, to confirm that these documents actually belong to you, you must submit a somesort of selfie holding these papers.

The point is that everything would be fine, but the EU places the responsibility for checking and storing the data from these checks on platform holders. Do you really want to hand over scans of your documents to entities that are constantly having data leak onto the web? Exactly.

It turns out a strange thing -- the EU says that this is your responsibility, but if you have a leak, they will come to punish you again. They came up with this cleverly! However, an important note -- perhaps the inspection data will be stored by some special office in Europe, and therefore the argument is invalid.
